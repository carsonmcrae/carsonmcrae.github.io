The question

Is a long session worth it?

TTSNative is a free, open-source (MIT) reader that turns documents into speech on your own computer. Its optional Voice Studio can clone your own voice from a quick 15–20 second recording, or build a studio voice from a long guided session.

My session was 48 short passages: about 30 minutes of my time for 7.6 minutes of audio. That is a lot to ask of anyone. Does a voice built from it sound more like the speaker than a quick clone?

What the code showed

What the engine actually uses.

Before testing anything, I traced the voice pipeline. The engine, Chatterbox Turbo (open source, MIT), is zero-shot: it copies a voice at the moment it speaks, and no model is trained on the recordings. When it speaks, it draws on:

  • 10 seconds of one take, for the voice's sound;
  • up to three takes per speaking style, for rhythm;
  • two speaker embeddings, numerical summaries of the speaker, averaged over all takes.

In the complete voice, the audio of only 14 of 42 takes was used. The averaged embeddings settle after about 8–12 takes. So the open question was whether the rest of the session added anything you could hear.

The experiment

Six voices, ten new lines, rated blind.

Six voices read ten items that no voice had read before. They used the app's real synthesis path with fixed seeds, and six of my takes were held out of every build.

  • Narration
  • Dialogue
  • A sad line
  • An excited line
  • A question
  • Unusual words
  • Names
  • Changes of style
  • A calm line
  • A 47-second passage

I rated every clip blind. The order of the voices was shuffled for each item. I scored each clip's likeness and naturalness from 1 to 5, marked garbled words, and picked the best clip for each item.

What it means

The setup mattered more than the length.

The careful recording setup made the big difference over a quick clone: the same good microphone, a quiet room and clean takes. Recording 48 passages instead of about 13 did not, and the small fine-tune didn't help yet.

One take from the session (A2) scored close to the early voice on likeness, but lowest of all on naturalness.

What I changed

Ask only for what the voice uses.

  • The studio session asks for about 15 suggested passages instead of 48, and points to the most useful one to record next.
  • The voice tiers say what they mean (Foundation, Developing and Complete), and that no model is trained.
  • A rebuilt expression can be switched back.
  • A blind listening panel tool makes this test repeatable.

These app changes are not in a release yet.

Listening notes

What the scores don't show.

Alongside the scores, I noted where the voices still fell short of a real reading:

  • Excited lines and questions lacked real inflection.
  • Every voice stumbled on words like gnocchi, quinoa and “Dr. Nguyen”.
  • Background static was present while speaking but cut to dead silence in the pauses.
  • Changes of style within a paragraph didn't flow, and calm lines sounded least like me.
  • An unquoted inner thought was read the same way as quoted speech.

Next steps

Fix what I heard, then test again.

  • Fill pauses with matching room tone.
  • Add better guidance and checks for expressive takes.
  • Use the pronunciation dictionary for unusual words.
  • Try a second engine or a better fine-tune, and adopt one only if a blind test shows it wins.

This was one listener and ten items: a careful first test, not a study.

What I took from it

The useful answer was a smaller ask. Instead of half an hour at the microphone, the studio can ask for what the voice actually uses, and the same blind test can check the next change.
